How to Get Chinese Documents Translated for Canadian Immigration

What Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada expects when your documents are in Chinese, and how to prepare them without delays.

31 October 2026 · 4 min read · TransWind

If you are applying to Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC) with documents in Chinese, every one of them needs an English or French translation. Getting the format wrong is one of the easiest ways to delay an application, so here is what IRCC asks for and how to prepare it from China.

1. What IRCC asks you to send

For each document that is not in English or French, IRCC's Express Entry instructions ask for three things (IRCC: Express Entry):

  • the English or French translation;
  • a copy of the original Chinese document, or a certified copy, that the translator worked from;
  • either a certified translator's stamp, or an affidavit from the translator.

Stamps and seals on the original that are in Chinese must be translated too.

2. Who can translate

IRCC accepts two routes.

A certified translator. For Express Entry, this means a member in good standing of a professional translation association in Canada or abroad. The translation should carry their seal or stamp with a membership number.

Any other translator, with an affidavit. The translator swears an affidavit, before a notary public, that the translation is accurate. Outside Canada this can be a notary public or equivalent. IRCC lists this as the option to use only if a certified translator is not available.

Some applications set slightly different rules. The citizenship certificate guide, for example, accepts a Canadian certified translator without an affidavit, and any other bilingual translator with one (IRCC: Guide CIT 0001). Always read the guide for your own application.

3. Who cannot translate

You, your family members, and your immigration representative or consultant cannot translate your documents, swear the affidavit, or certify your copies. IRCC does not accept translations by family members (IRCC Help Centre).

4. Documents people usually need from China

  • Birth, marriage and divorce certificates, and household register (hukou) pages
  • Police certificates (no-criminal-record certificates)
  • Diplomas, degree certificates and transcripts
  • Employment letters, bank statements and proof of funds

Some civil documents, such as birth or marriage records, are often issued by Chinese notary offices as notarial certificates. Check whether your application asks for these.

5. A simple checklist

  • Find the document list for your exact application on IRCC's website.
  • Collect clear scans of every page, including the back and every stamp.
  • Choose your route: a certified translator, or a translator plus an affidavit.
  • Keep names, dates and spellings the same across all your translations and forms.
  • Upload the translation together with the copy of the original it was made from.
